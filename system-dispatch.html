FAQ

Common Questions

What does TMI's AI dispatch system do?

TMI's dispatch system routes incoming jobs to the optimal technician automatically - matching by skill, certification, current location, and availability without a human dispatcher making phone calls. Standard daily dispatching runs without intervention. Emergency escalation rules handle complex calls.

How is autonomous dispatch different from a scheduling tool?

A scheduling tool shows available slots and lets a dispatcher assign jobs. Autonomous dispatch reads live data - tech location, job status, certification matrix, urgency - and makes the assignment automatically. The difference is whether a human is in the loop for each job or only for exceptions.

What happens when a tech calls in sick or a job runs long?

The system reads the change in real time and adjusts downstream assignments automatically. If a tech becomes unavailable, their jobs are redistributed by the same routing logic. If a job runs long, subsequent assignments shift without a dispatcher manually reworking the board.

How does the certification matching work in dispatch?

The system maintains a certification matrix for each technician - license class, specialty certifications, equipment authorizations. When a job requires a specific certification, only techs who hold it are eligible for assignment. This eliminates both liability exposure from incorrect dispatch and the liability exposure from a dispatcher who doesn't know every tech's certification status.

How much does autonomous dispatch reduce daily dispatch time?

TMI's documented outcome is dispatch time reduced by 3-6 hours per day for companies that previously handled dispatching manually. That time shifts to exception handling - complex jobs, VIP clients, emergency responses - rather than routine call assignment.

How long does dispatch automation implementation take?

Dispatch automation typically deploys in 4-8 weeks, including integration with existing job management systems and configuration of routing rules, certification matrices, and escalation protocols.
