TMI Tech AI · Field Sales Manual

The City Leads Playbook.

You work three channels: walk-ins as your bread and butter, social media to warm the market, and outbound to fill the gaps. Two things win in all of them: you can explain what TMI Tech AI does in plain English, and you become the local person an owner trusts on this.

Your one job. Find owners of established companies in your city who are buried in their own business, and sell them the Intelligent Company Audit, which is $5,000. You send a tagged payment link by text or email, or the owner scans your QR. If the owner is not ready to buy, book the free Fit Call with Mia and Tyler, or set a dated follow-up. Never leave without a person, an action, and a date. You do not quote builds and you do not promise results. Who we're for, and who we're not

You'll waste your week talking to the wrong people. TMI Tech AI is built for established industrial and family businesses with a real operation: manufacturers, machine shops, oil and gas, construction, logistics and fleet, field service, marine, and equipment. The owner, and the son or daughter taking it over. Outside industrial only when it is a genuine fit. You spot a good lead by what's breaking, not only by industry.

Green flags (go after)

  • Established, with employees, customers, and more than one system
  • Owner answers every question and works long hours
  • Crews, trucks, jobs, multiple sites
  • Runs on spreadsheets, texts, whiteboards, one overworked office manager
  • A succession or handover coming in the family
  • Says "I can't take a vacation" or "it all runs through me"

Red flags (skip)

  • Pre-revenue, or a solo operator with no staff
  • Pure e-commerce or software, no operations
  • Already systematized, running hands-off
  • Owner closing the doors, not handing over or selling
  • Wants free consulting with no intent to buy
  • Not a decision maker, no path to one

Walk-ins: your bread and butter

Walking into a business is our highest-trust channel. You're a real local person, in the flesh. Owners in the trades respect that. Your goal on a walk-in is to get a few minutes with the owner and either sell the audit or book the Fit Call, or get their name and cell to follow up on a set date.

Before you walk in

  • Know the business. Name, what they do, and the owner's name if you can find it.
  • Look the part. Clean, local, approachable. A peer, not a suit.
  • Carry the leave-behind. The TMI Tech AI one-pager and your tagged QR for the audit. Never empty-handed.
  • Work a route. Cluster visits so you hit 15 to 25 in a run.

The five moves, every time

  1. Warm open with whoever's at the front. Smile, relaxed, brief. The front desk decides if you reach the owner, so treat them well.
  2. Ask for the owner by name. "Is [Owner] around by chance?" Casual, like you belong there.
  3. If the owner's there: 30-second pitch, read the room, ask for the audit. Not ready? Ask for the Fit Call.
  4. If not there: get their name and best cell, leave the one-pager, get the gatekeeper on your side. Don't just drop a flyer.
  5. Same-day follow-up text. Every walk-in gets a text that afternoon, met them or not.

Walk-in scripts

Owner is there (30-sec pitch + ask)"[Owner], I'll be quick, I know you're busy. I'm [Rep], local, with TMI Tech AI. We go inside established companies, find where time, knowledge and visibility are being lost, and build the systems around what already works. On a walk-in, do

The psychology of trust

In person, you're earning trust fast enough that the owner will give you a real conversation. Two well-known frameworks help you do that on purpose, not by accident.

The Trust Equation (Maister)

A simple model from David Maister's work on why people trust advisors. Learn it, then aim every interaction at it.

TRUST = ( Credibility + Reliability + Intimacy ) ÷ Self-OrientationThe two that matter most in the field are Intimacy and low Self-Orientation, not your credentials.
  • Credibility. You know their world. Speak their language, reference real operations (this is Module 2). It's the price of entry, not the winner.
  • Reliability. You do what you say. Show up when you said, send what you promised, follow up on time. Small promises kept build this fast.
  • Intimacy. They feel safe telling you the real problem. You get there by asking, listening, and being a normal human.
  • Self-Orientation. The divider, and the most important. The rep who clearly wants to help more than they want the sale wins. Lower it out loud: "If this isn't a fit, I'll tell you." That one line does more than any close.

Cialdini's principles, on a walk-in

Robert Cialdini's seven levers of influence. Use them honestly. Never fake them.

PrincipleHow you use it
ReciprocityGive first. A genuine tip, an intro, something useful about their own business.
CommitmentGet a small yes (a free Fit Call) that can lead to the audit.
Social proofOnly ever say what's true. TMI publishes no case studies, so never name a client or attach a result to one.
AuthoritySignal you know operations: look the part, speak with competence, use the brand.
LikingSimilarity, genuine compliments, cooperation. Find real common ground.
ScarcityOnly if it is true and you have been told it is. Never invent urgency.
UnityShared identity: "we work with owners like you, local, in the trenches." Be on their side.

What your body is saying

Owners read you in the first few seconds, from tone and posture as much as words. So:

  • Open posture, firm handshake, easy eye contact, lean slightly in. Confident and calm, never rushed.
  • Mirror and pace, subtly. Match their energy and speed. Fast talker, pick it up. Slow and thoughtful, slow down. It makes people feel understood.
  • Keep it natural. Forced or obvious mirroring backfires, and you never mirror anger or frustration, that just escalates it.
  • Read the room. If they're slammed and tense, the trust move is to respect it and offer to come back on a set day.
10

Read the person: sell to their style

People buy in different languages. The classic model (Merrill-Reid social styles, close to the four DISC types) sorts people by two things: how fast and direct they are, and how people-focused they are. You can usually get a read in the first minute and adjust on the fly. Many trades owners are Drivers, so default to brief and direct until you learn otherwise.

Driver

Wants to know: WHAT · fast & in charge
Spot it: blunt, impatient, "get to the point," runs the room.
Sell it: be brief, say what they get and what it costs, give 2 options, let them decide.
Avoid: small talk, long stories, over-explaining.

Analytical

Wants to know: HOW · careful & precise
Spot it: asks detailed questions, cautious, wants proof, not emotional.
Sell it: walk through how the audit runs and what the report contains, don't rush, send the audit page, let them think.
Avoid: hype, pressure, vague claims.

Amiable

Wants to know: WHY · warm & relationship-first
Spot it: friendly, easygoing, avoids conflict, cares who you are.
Sell it: build trust first, be gentle, guide. The free Fit Call with the founders often suits them.
Avoid: aggressive closing, rushing, pushiness.

Expressive

Wants to know: WHO · high energy & big-picture
Spot it: talkative, enthusiastic, tells stories, loves the vision.
Sell it: match their energy, talk about the next version of their company, keep it moving.
Avoid: dry data dumps, killing the energy with fine print.
The move: most people are a blend. Don't type them and forget it. Read their energy early, start in that style, and keep adjusting to how they respond. Same offer, different delivery.
11

Become the go-to

Here's the whole game. You are not a one-shot salesperson. You are becoming the person in your city an owner thinks of first when they think "I need this business to stop running through me." Many owners you meet aren't ready today. The rep who stays useful and keeps a dated follow-up on them wins them later, and gets their referrals too.

How you become the go-to

  • Give value with no ask. Send a useful letter, a tip, an intro, even when they don't buy. Reciprocity plus low self-orientation, on repeat.
  • Follow through, every time. Do the small things you said you'd do. Reliability is how a stranger becomes a trusted name.
  • Teach, don't sell. Be the person who explains intelligent companies in plain English. The person who explains it clearly gets the call.
  • Stay in their world. Show up at their spots, know their industry, talk like an insider. You're one of them, not a vendor passing through.
  • Bring insight. Tell an owner something true about their own business they hadn't seen. That's what a trusted advisor does.
  • Work the "not now" list. Every "not now" gets a dated follow-up, not an open-ended "I'll check in."
  • Earn referrals. Owners talk to owners. Once you've earned it, ask: "Who else do you know that's buried in their business?"
Own the identity out loud: "I'm the person in [City] who helps [industry] owners build intelligent companies, with TMI Tech AI." Every rep who becomes the go-to person is how TMI Tech AI becomes the go-to name.
12

What to ask

Once you have a live conversation, in any channel, your job is not to talk. Let the owner talk about 70% of the time. Get them saying the problem out loud, in their words. These map straight to what the audit looks at.

  • "What made this conversation worth taking now?"
  • "Walk me through what still runs through you personally every day."
  • "If you took thirty days off with no phone, what breaks first?"
  • "Where do jobs or leads fall through the cracks most often?"
  • "What number do you wish you could see every morning but can't?"
  • "Who are the one or two people you couldn't run without, and why?"
  • "What have you already tried to fix this, and what happened?"
  • "What happens if nothing changes for twelve months?"

The pivot: "Everything you just described is what the Intelligent Company Audit looks at. You'd get a score, a five-page report, and a roadmap you keep. Want me to text you the link, or would you rather talk to the founders on a free Fit Call first?"

13

Objections, answered

Objections aren't rejection, they're the conversation. Acknowledge, clarify, answer, confirm, then ask again. Never argue, and never answer an objection the owner did not raise.

They say"We already have software for that."
"Good, most owners do. Usually the problem isn't one more tool, it's that the tools don't talk and people hold it together. Preserve what works, modernize what doesn't. The audit shows which is which."
They say"Is this an AI thing?"
"No. We're not an AI agency. AI is one tool, and it's often not the first thing a company needs. We work on the company itself: operations, systems, brand, and growth. The audit decides what you actually need."
They say"Why isn't the audit free?"
"Because a free audit is a sales call wearing a costume. Paying for it means we go into the systems and the numbers, and you own a roadmap you can hand to a partner, a banker or a buyer. The conversation about whether we're a fit is free. That's the Fit Call."
They say"$5,000 is a lot."
"Fair. It's a fixed price for a fixed thing: a score out of 100 across ten areas, a five-page report, and the roadmap, which is yours whatever you decide. If you want to be sure first, talk to the founders on the free Fit Call. Want me to set that up?"
They say"What would the work after cost?"
"Builds start around fifteen thousand. What it actually costs depends on how many departments it touches, how much has to be built versus connected, and how much knowledge has to come out of people's heads. You'd get a real number in writing after the audit, not before."
They say"I don't have time for this."
"That's usually the sign. The audit is 30 to 45 minutes, in person or by phone, and the Fit Call is 20. What does next week look like?"
They say"Just leave some info."
"Happy to, and I will. The audit page lays out exactly what you get and what it costs. Can I follow up Thursday to see if you want the audit or the Fit Call?"
They say"We tried automation, it didn't work."
"We hear that, usually because a tool got bolted onto a process nobody mapped. We come on site and follow the work first, then build with the people who'll use it. The audit is where we find what went sideways last time."
They say"I need to talk to my partner."
"Makes sense. The Fit Call works well with both of you on it, and the roadmap is something you'd both want to read. Should we book a time that works for them too?"
They say"Call me in a few months."
"Can do. What changes in a few months? Let's put a date on it now so I'm not guessing."
They say"Not interested."
"Fair enough. Can I ask, is it the timing, or is getting the business to run without you just not a priority right now? (Listen. Their answer tells you whether to set a dated follow-up or bow out.)"

Selling the audit & making it stick

When they lean in, don't oversell. Ask directly and handle the logistics on the spot.

  • Ask directly. "Want me to text you the link for the audit?" Not "would you maybe want to."
  • Send the tagged link. Text, email, or let them scan your QR while you're there. It has to be your tagged link so you are credited.
  • Not ready to buy? Two options. "Free Fit Call with the founders, Tuesday morning or Thursday afternoon?"
  • Confirm decision makers. "Anyone else who'd want to be on it?"
  • Set the frame. "Nothing to prepare. They research the company beforehand."
  • Lock the cell. Confirm the best number for confirmations and reminders.
  • Never commit TMI to a timeline, scope, discount, or start date.
Show-up defense. The booking system sends reminders. On top of that, send a personal text the morning of: "Looking forward to it at [time], [Name]. Anything you want to make sure gets covered?" People show up for people.

Voice & rules

How we say things is part of the product. You represent a company that sells order, so sound ordered: calm, plain, specific, never pushy or hypey.

Say this

  • "TMI Tech AI," "intelligent company," "the next version of your company"
  • "Intelligent Company Audit," "$5,000," "the roadmap is yours"
  • "Fit Call," "free, 15 minutes"
  • "Lafayette, Louisiana. On site nationwide."
  • The owner's own numbers, clearly framed as their example

Never say

  • "AI agency," "AI company," "bots," "chatbots"
  • "Leverage AI," "cutting-edge," "revolutionary," hype words
