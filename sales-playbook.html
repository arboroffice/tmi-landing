TMI Technology · Field Sales Manual

The City Leads Playbook.

You work three channels: walk-ins as your bread and butter, social media to warm the market, and outbound to fill the gaps. Two things win in all of them: you can explain what TMI does and how it helps in plain English, and you make yourself the local person an owner trusts on this. Do that and you don't chase deals. Owners come to you.

Your one job. Find owners in your city who are buried in their own business, make them interested in what TMI does, and book them onto the free Business Intelligence Audit, a call with Mia to go deeper and run the intelligent audit. You do not close. You do not quote price. You do not explain every detail. You spark interest and book the call. Foundations first, then the three channels, then the human side (this is what makes you great), then the conversation and how to run your week. Read it once end to end. Then live in Modules 2, 6, 9, and 11.
01

What TMI is, in your sleep

You cannot sell what you cannot say in one breath. TMI builds intelligent companies. We take an operation that runs on people, memory, and a dozen disconnected apps, and we install the systems, the digital workforce, and the command center the business runs on instead. The owner stops being the bottleneck. The company starts running without them.

The one-liner (10 seconds)

"We help established [industry] owners get out of the day-to-day by building the systems their business runs on, so it grows without depending on them."

The 30-second version

"Most owners doing a few million a year become the system. Every decision, every fire, every answer runs through them, and growth just makes it heavier. We redesign how the operation runs, install the backend that holds it together, and put the whole business on one screen. Built in about 30 days, and you own it. It always starts with a free audit of where your time and money are leaking."

The belief (why now)

"The companies that win the next decade won't be the ones with the most people. They'll be the ones with the best systems, data, and intelligence behind every move. Every company eventually becomes an intelligent company. The only question is who in your market builds it first."

03

The offer, and how we make money

You lead with one thing only: the free Business Intelligence Audit. It's the front door to everything. You're not selling a build on the street. You're giving away a genuinely valuable, genuinely free diagnosis.

What the free audit actually is

It's a 20-minute call with Mia, our founder, who has built and scaled these companies herself. We research the company, map how it runs today, score every part of the business, and hand the owner a 30-day plan showing exactly where time and money are leaking and what it's costing them. Free. Your strongest line: "Worst case, you get a roadmap."

What happens after (so you sound credible)

  • The install. We build their operating system, digital workforce, and command center in about 30 days. They own it.
  • The growth partner model. For the right owner, we embed as their growth team and tie our pay to the value we create. Less vendor, more partner.
The price rule. Memorize this. You never quote a price. Price depends entirely on what the audit uncovers, and the audit does the selling. If they push: "That's exactly what the audit answers. It's free, and it tells us both whether there's even a fit before anyone talks dollars."
04

Who we're for, and who we're not

You'll waste your week talking to the wrong people. We fit operations-heavy companies, roughly $2M and up, where the owner is still the glue. Industrial, trades, home service, and oil and gas mainly, but we work with all kinds of companies. You spot a good lead by what's breaking, not by industry.

Green flags (go after)

  • $2M+ and growing, but chaos grew with it
  • Owner answers every question, works 60+ hours
  • Crews, trucks, jobs, multiple sites
  • Runs on spreadsheets, texts, whiteboards, one overworked office manager
  • Recently hired a dispatcher or "data entry" person to keep up
  • Says "I can't take a vacation" or "it all runs through me"

Red flags (skip)

  • Under ~$1M, or a solo operator
  • Pure e-commerce or software, no operations
  • Already systematized, running hands-off
  • Owner retiring next quarter, coasting
  • Tire-kicker who wants free consulting
  • Not a decision maker, no path to one

06

Walk-ins: your bread and butter

Walking into a business is our highest-trust channel. You're a real local person, in the flesh, offering something free. Owners in the trades respect that. Your goal on a walk-in is rarely to close, it's to get 60 seconds with the owner and book the audit, or get their name and cell to follow up.

Before you walk in

  • Know the business. Name, what they do, and the owner's name if you can find it.
  • Look the part. Clean, local, approachable. A peer, not a suit.
  • Carry the leave-behind. The TMI one-pager with the QR that books the audit. Never empty-handed.
  • Work a route. Cluster visits so you hit 15 to 25 in a run.

The five moves, every time

  1. Warm open with whoever's at the front. Smile, relaxed, brief. The front desk decides if you reach the owner, so treat them like gold.
  2. Ask for the owner by name. "Is [Owner] around by chance?" Casual, like you belong there.
  3. If the owner's there: 30-second in-person pitch, read the room, ask for the audit.
  4. If not there: get their name and best cell, leave the one-pager, get the gatekeeper on your side. Don't just drop a flyer.
  5. Same-day follow-up text. Every walk-in gets a text that afternoon, met them or not.

Walk-in scripts

To the gatekeeper"Hey, how's it going? I'm [Rep], I'm local, I work with TMI. We help [industry] owners around here get out of the day-to-day with better systems. Is [Owner] around for two minutes, or is there a better time to catch them?"
Owner is there (30-sec pitch + ask)"[Owner], I'll be quick, I know you're busy. I'm [Rep], local, with TMI. We help established [industry] owners build the systems the business runs on so it stops running through you. We're doing a few free operational audits for [City] companies this month, we map where your time and money are leaking and hand you a 30-day plan. Worst case you get a roadmap. Can I grab 20 minutes on your calendar next week?"
Owner not there"No worries. What's the best way to reach [Owner], a cell that gets to them directly? I'll leave this too. It's a free operational audit we're running for a few [City] companies. Can you make sure it gets in front of them? Really appreciate it, [gatekeeper's name]."
Same-day text (missed them)Hi [Owner], [Rep] with TMI. Swung by [Company] today and missed you. We're doing free operational audits for a few [City] companies, mapping where time and money leak. Worth 20 min? Happy to work around your schedule.

On a walk-in, do

  • Respect their time, read the room, keep it under a minute
  • Win the gatekeeper first
  • Always leave the one-pager and get a name + cell
  • Log every visit the same day

On a walk-in, don't

  • Pitch the whole thing at the counter
  • Talk over a busy shop, offer to come back
  • Leave a flyer and walk without a conversation
  • Get pushy, or take a "not today" personally

Social media: warm the market

Social isn't for hard selling, it's for credibility and warm outreach. When an owner looks you up or gets your DM, your profile should make you look like a real, helpful local who knows this world. Social turns cold into warm, and warm into a booked audit.

What to post and share

  • TMI's Founders of the Future Letters and short clips about getting out of the day-to-day.
  • Plain-language posts: "What an intelligent company actually looks like," "3 things costing [industry] owners money every week."
  • Local flavor: businesses you've visited (with permission), your day in the field, wins.
  • The free-audit offer, framed as value, not a pitch.

DM outreach (the real lever)

Opening DM (warm)Hey [Name], love what you've built at [Company]. I work with [industry] owners around [City] on getting the business to run without them being the bottleneck. Not pitching you, genuinely think you'd get value from a free operational audit we're running. Open to me sending details?
After they replyAwesome. Quick version: we map where your time and money are leaking and hand you a 30-day plan. Free, about 20 min. Want me to grab a time or send the link?
Rule: value first, always. If your whole feed is "book a call," people tune out. A handful of real conversations a day beats a hundred blasts.

09

The psychology of trust

In person, you're not selling a product in the first meeting. You're earning trust fast enough that they'll give you 20 minutes. Two proven frameworks make you good at this on purpose, not by accident.

The Trust Equation (Maister)

Decades of research on why people trust advisors boils down to one formula. Learn it, then aim every interaction at it.

TRUST = ( Credibility + Reliability + Intimacy ) ÷ Self-OrientationThe two that move the needle most are Intimacy and low Self-Orientation, not your credentials.
  • Credibility — you know their world. Speak their language, reference real operations (this is Module 2). It's the price of entry, not the winner.
  • Reliability — you do what you say. Show up when you said, send what you promised, follow up on time. Small promises kept build this fast.
  • Intimacy — they feel safe telling you the real problem. You get there by asking, listening, and being a normal human, not a pitch machine.
  • Self-Orientation — the divider, and the most important. The rep who clearly wants to help more than they want the sale wins. Lower it out loud: "If this isn't a fit, I'll tell you." That one line beats any close.

Cialdini's principles, on a walk-in

The seven research-backed levers of influence. Use them honestly. Never fake them.

PrincipleHow you use it
ReciprocityGive first. The free audit, a genuine tip, an intro. People feel the pull to give back.
CommitmentGet a small yes (20-minute audit) that leads to bigger yeses. Start tiny.
Social proof"Other [industry] owners around here are doing this." Only ever say what's true.
AuthoritySignal you know operations: look the part, speak with competence, use the brand.
LikingSimilarity, genuine compliments, cooperation. Find real common ground.
Scarcity"We're only doing a few audits in [City] this month." True capacity, not fake urgency.
UnityShared identity: "we work with owners like you, local, in the trenches." Be on their side.

What your body is saying

Most of how an owner reads your trustworthiness in the first seconds is nonverbal, tone and posture more than words. So:

  • Open posture, firm handshake, easy eye contact, lean slightly in. Confident and calm, never rushed.
  • Mirror and pace, subtly. Match their energy and speed. Fast talker, pick it up. Slow and thoughtful, slow down. It makes people feel understood.
  • Keep it natural. Forced or obvious mirroring backfires, and you never mirror anger or frustration, that just escalates it.
  • Read the room. If they're slammed and tense, the trust move is to respect it and offer to come back.
10

Read the person: sell to their style

People buy in different languages. The classic model (Merrill-Reid social styles, the same four as DISC) sorts owners into four types by two things: how fast and direct they are, and how people-focused they are. You can peg most owners in 30 seconds and adjust on the fly. Many trades owners are Drivers, so default to brief and direct until you learn otherwise.

Driver

Wants to know: WHAT · fast & in charge
Spot it: blunt, impatient, "get to the point," runs the room.
Sell it: be brief, lead with the result, give 2 options, respect their time, let them decide.
Avoid: small talk, long stories, over-explaining.

Analytical

Wants to know: HOW · careful & precise
Spot it: asks detailed questions, cautious, wants proof, not emotional.
Sell it: give specifics and process, don't rush, offer to send info, let them think.
Avoid: hype, pressure, vague claims.

Amiable

Wants to know: WHY · warm & relationship-first
Spot it: friendly, easygoing, avoids conflict, cares who you are.
Sell it: build trust first, be gentle, reassure, guide. They buy from people they like.
Avoid: aggressive closing, rushing, pushiness.

Expressive

Wants to know: WHO · high energy & big-picture
Spot it: talkative, enthusiastic, tells stories, loves the vision.
Sell it: match their energy, paint the picture, get excited with them, keep it moving.
Avoid: dry data dumps, killing the vibe with fine print.
The move: most people are a blend. Don't type them and forget it. Read their energy in the first 30 seconds, start in that style, and keep adjusting to how they respond. Same pitch, different delivery.
11

Become the go-to

Here's the whole game. You are not a one-shot salesperson. You are becoming the person in your city an owner thinks of first when they think "I need to get my business to run without me." Most owners you meet aren't ready today. The rep who stays useful and top-of-mind wins them in three, six, twelve months, and gets their referrals too.

How you become the go-to

  • Give value with no ask. Send a useful article, a tip, an intro, even when they don't book. Reciprocity plus low self-orientation, on repeat.
  • Follow through, every time. Do the small things you said you'd do. Reliability is how a stranger becomes a trusted name.
  • Teach, don't sell. Be the person who explains intelligent systems in plain English. Post it, say it, show it. The expert gets the call.
  • Stay in their world. Show up at their spots, know their industry, talk like an insider. You're one of them, not a vendor passing through.
  • Bring insight. Tell an owner something true about their own business they hadn't seen. That's what a trusted advisor does, and it's unforgettable.
  • Work the "not now" list. A helpful check-in every month or two. When they're finally ready, or a buddy asks "who do I talk to about this," you're the answer.
  • Earn referrals. Owners talk to owners. Be so clear and helpful that they send you people. Once you've earned it, ask: "Who else do you know that's buried in their business?"
Own the identity out loud: "I'm the person in [City] who helps [industry] owners build intelligent companies." Say it, live it, and it becomes true. Every rep who becomes the go-to person is how TMI becomes the go-to name.
12

What to ask

Once you have a live conversation, in any channel, your job is not to talk. It's to get them saying the pain out loud, in their words. Ask, then listen. These map straight to what the audit diagnoses.

  • "Walk me through what still runs through you personally every day."
  • "If you took two weeks off with no phone, what breaks first?"
  • "Where do jobs or leads fall through the cracks most often?"
  • "How do you know today if you're at, over, or under capacity?"
  • "What number do you wish you could see every morning but can't?"
  • "Who are the one or two people you couldn't run without, and why?"
  • "What have you already tried to fix this, and what happened?"

The pivot to booking: "Everything you just described is exactly what the audit maps. Let's get it on the calendar and show you what it's actually costing you."

13

Objections, answered

Objections aren't rejection, they're the conversation. Acknowledge, reframe, redirect to the free audit. Never argue.

They say"We already have software for that."
"Perfect, most owners we work with do. The problem is rarely one more tool, it's that they don't talk and you're holding it together. The audit shows exactly where that's costing you. It's free."
They say"Is this an AI thing?"
"There's smart tech under the hood, but we don't sell you AI. We build the systems and digital workforce your business runs on and hand you the outcome. The audit shows what that looks like for your operation."
They say"I don't have time for this."
"That's exactly the reason to do it. If you had time you wouldn't need us. It's 20 minutes, and it's the one thing that could give you time back. What's next week look like?"
They say"What's it cost?"
"The audit's free. What a build costs depends on what it uncovers, which is why we start there. It tells us both whether there's even a fit before anyone talks dollars. Fair?"
They say"Just leave some info."
"Happy to, and I will. Honestly though the flyer's generic and your operation isn't. The audit's the version built around your actual business, same 20 minutes it'd take to read a PDF. Let's just book it."
They say"We tried automation, it didn't work."
"Hear that a lot, usually because someone bolted a tool onto a broken process. We map how you actually run first, then build around it. The audit's where we find what went sideways last time."
They say"We're slammed right now."
"Then you're exactly who this is for. Busy and buried is the problem we fix. We'll book it out a couple weeks, on your terms. Mornings or afternoons better?"
They say"I need to talk to my partner."
"Makes total sense. The audit's the perfect thing to bring them, the whole picture on one page. Let's book it and look together. Should they be on it too?"
They say"Call me in a few months."
"Can do. Quick question, what changes in a few months? The audit's free and gives you the plan now, so when you're ready you're not starting from zero. Want me to grab a slot?"
They say"Not interested."
"Fair enough, I won't be that guy. Can I ask, is it the timing, or is getting the business to run without you just not a priority right now? (Listen. Their answer tells you whether to book or bow out.)"
14

Booking the audit & making it stick

When they lean in, don't oversell. Assume the booking and lock the calendar.

  • Assume it. "Great, let's get you on the calendar." Not "would you maybe want to."
  • Two options. "Tuesday morning or Thursday afternoon?" Two yeses, not a yes/no.
  • Confirm decision makers. "Anyone else who'd want to be on it?"
  • Set the frame. "About 20 minutes. Come as you are, no prep. We do the digging."
  • Lock the cell. Confirm the best number for reminders. This kills no-shows.
  • Text the confirmation on the spot, while you're still with them.
No-show defense. The system sends automatic reminder texts and emails at 24 hours and 2 hours before. On top of that, send a personal text the morning of: "Looking forward to it at [time], [Name]. Anything you want to make sure we cover?" People show up for people.

Pre-audit: prep the owner, prep the team

A booked audit isn't done, it's loaded. The gap between booking and the call is where you set it up to convert. Two jobs: get the owner ready, and hand the team a clean brief.

Prep the owner

  • Set expectations. "20 minutes, no prep, we do the digging. Just be honest about where it hurts."
  • Confirm who's on it. Owner and any partner or right-hand who helps decide.
  • Note what you already know so we don't ask what we can look up: name, website, rough size, what they run on, the pain in their words.
  • Tee up the value. "You'll walk out with a real picture of where the money's leaking, whether we ever work together or not."

Why it matters: the closer walks in already knowing the owner's real pain, their style, and how to frame the fix. A warm, well-briefed handoff is the difference between an audit that converts and a cold restart.

18

Voice & rules

How we say things is part of the product. You represent a company that sells order, so sound ordered: calm, direct, confident, never pushy or hypey.

Say this

  • "Intelligent company," "the systems your business runs on"
  • "Digital workforce," "a department you hire"
  • "Free Business Intelligence Audit," "roadmap," "worst case you get a plan"
  • "Get out of the day-to-day," "runs without you"
  • Real numbers and specific scenarios from their world

Never say

  • "AI agents," "bots," "chatbots"
  • "Leverage AI," "cutting-edge," "revolutionary," hype words
  • Any price or dollar figure. The audit does that.
  • Promises we can't keep, or fake urgency
  • Talking over them. Ask, then listen.

Three habits that separate great reps: they log every visit and call so the next touch is smarter, they tell the truth even when it costs a booking (low self-orientation), and they never chase a bad fit just to hit a number.

19

Your first four weeks

You're not expected to be great on day one. You're expected to ramp on this schedule.

  • Week 1 — Learn. Master Modules 1, 2, and 6. Learn to explain us cold. Shadow walk-in routes. First 10 walk-ins by Friday.
  • Week 2 — Reps. Full activity across all three channels. Book your first audit. Role-play objections and the four personality types daily.
  • Week 3 — Refine. Review your numbers, find your weakest step, fix it. Practice reading the person and lowering self-orientation.
  • Week 4 — Own it. Consistent booked audits, clean handoffs, a worked pipeline, and a growing "go-to" list. Certified.

Certification checklist

  • I can explain what we do and how we help in plain English, with examples.
  • I can give the one-liner, 30-second pitch, and the belief without notes.
  • I can run a walk-in: win the gatekeeper, pitch the owner, book or get a cell.
  • I can start warm conversations on social without spamming.
  • I aim every interaction at the Trust Equation and lower my self-orientation out loud.
  • I can read the four personality types in 30 seconds and adjust my delivery.
  • I have a word-for-word answer to all ten objections.
  • I book with two-option assumptive closes and confirm decision makers.
  • I prep the owner and fill out a clean handoff brief every time.
  • I give value with no ask and keep a real "go-to" list warm.
  • I never quote price and never say "AI agents."
  • I've booked and handed off my first qualified audit.
