TMI Brand Account Plan
The Network.
Not the Show.

TMI is not a brand account. It is a media network for people who build, fix, and run physical operations. Every post, skit, and story is sourced from the field and built to make the right people feel seen.

What the TMI Account Is

A distribution channel, a recruiting tool, and a proof-of-work engine - running at the same time. When someone watches TMI content, they should feel like they found the people who understand their world. Not a tech company explaining the trades. Not a consulting firm doing thought leadership. The operators.

The account runs on three pillars: Real footage from real operations. Skits that make field operators laugh and nod. Results that make owners pick up the phone.

What We Make
The Five Content Types

Every piece of TMI content is one of five types. Mix these across the week. Never let the account go more than three days without an IN THE FIELD or SKIT post.

Type 01
IN THE FIELD
Raw, real footage from job sites and operations. Truck cabs, dispatch floors, equipment yards. The world most content doesn't show.
Type 02
THE BUILD
How the AI systems get built. Screen recordings, walkthroughs, the before and after of a real client operation going from paper to automated.
Type 03
THE RESULT
Client wins, transformations, real numbers. What changed in the first 30 days. What the owner said when billing automated and cash flow moved.
Type 04
THE MOVEMENT
Culture, positioning, manifesto content. Who this is for. What we're building. Why the trades deserve better than generic SaaS sold by people who never dispatched a crew.
Type 05
THE SKITS
Scripted short-form comedy and drama about the real experience of running a trades or field service company. Makes operators laugh because it is exactly their situation.
Scripted Content
The Skit Series

Recurring characters, recurring situations. Build audience by making the same people laugh about a different version of the same problem every week.

Series A
City Lead vs. Sales Rep
A TMI city lead who knows every HVAC owner in the metro vs. a startup SaaS sales rep who just wants to book a demo. Same world, completely different relationship to it. Recurring characters, escalating tension each episode. The city lead never pitches. The sales rep never stops.
Format: 30-60s skit · Recurring characters · Comedy
Series B
AI Did It While I Was...
Owner is doing something completely unrelated - fishing, asleep, at their kid's soccer game - and gets a notification that a job was dispatched, an invoice collected, or a renewal locked. Cut to what would have taken three phone calls and a dispatcher's afternoon. Tone: disbelief shading into joy.
Format: 15-30s · Relatable moment · Automation win
Series C
Business Owner Bingo
Bingo card format - squares are things every field service owner has said, heard, or dealt with. "We've always done it this way." "The guys won't use it." "We need to hire another dispatcher." When the owner calls Bingo, they call TMI. Run as a recurring carousel or short-form video with a punchline ending.
Format: Carousel or short video · Relatable pain · CTA punchline
Series D
What TMI Does, Explained To...
Same explanation of TMI delivered to completely different people: a 70-year-old plumbing company owner, a 24-year-old city lead, a VC who doesn't understand field service, a dispatcher worried about their job, a foreman who's heard "we're getting AI" before. Same concept, completely different conversation every episode.
Format: 30-60s · Recurring concept · New character per episode

Content Strategy
Funnel Breakdown

Every piece of content is assigned a funnel layer before it goes live. If 80% of what you post is TOF, the audience grows but doesn't convert. If 80% is BOF, you're pitching to people who aren't warm yet.

Top of Funnel
50%
Awareness & reach
Middle of Funnel
35%
Trust & education
Bottom of Funnel
15%
Conversion & CTA
TOF - 50%
Skits, field footage, relatable moments. Hook new people. No pitch. No ask. Just content worth watching and sharing.
MOF - 35%
Build walkthroughs, before/after results, field data, operator stories. Build trust and show exactly how the work gets done.
BOF - 15%
Specific ROI proof, application CTAs, testimonials with numbers. One in seven posts should ask for something. Not more.
The Strategy
The Netflix Model

Netflix doesn't publish random movies and hope people come back. It builds franchises, recurring characters, and serialized worlds. Audiences return because they know the next episode is coming.

TMI runs on the same model. Instead of posting individual pieces of content, we build recurring series that people follow. City Lead Diaries gives them a character. Business Owner Bingo gives them something to share. AI Did It While I Was gives them something to screenshot and send their dispatcher.

When someone discovers TMI, they don't find one good post. They find an archive of a world they want to live in. That is the retention play. One-off viral content builds reach. Serialized content builds audience. The goal is both.

Recurring Programming
Series on the TMI Account

Six anchored series run simultaneously. Mix through the week. Each builds a separate audience segment that overlaps on the TMI brand.

Series 01
The Build Series
Full walkthroughs of real client builds. From the intake call to the first automated invoice. What the system looked like, what the owner said, what changed in week one.
Series 02
City Lead Diaries
A week in the life of a TMI city lead. Meetings, cold walks into shops, driving the market, what they're hearing from operators. Real footage, real conversations, no script.
Series 03
The Unsexy Business Series
Features on the unglamorous side of field operations - dispatch at 5am, the invoice that bounced, the crew that went to the wrong site. What these moments cost and how to stop them.
Series 04
Before and After
Clean split format. What the operation looked like before TMI. What it looks like now. Numbers where possible. Owner on camera where they're willing. Raw and honest.
Series 05
AI Did It While I Was...
Short-form recurring skit. Owner is somewhere else entirely. The AI handles the business. Cut together as: what the AI did vs. what the owner was doing at the same moment. 15-30 seconds.
Series 06
Business Owner Bingo
Recurring relatable pain format. New bingo card each episode with fresh phrases from the field. Carousel or short video. Ends with the punchline and the CTA.
The Crossover Episode

Once a quarter: a City Lead from Diaries meets a client from Before and After. Or a build from the Build Series becomes the result in an Unsexy Business Episode. Crossovers signal depth - the audience rewards it because it proves the universe is real. Every crossover gets a timestamp callback to the earlier episode.

Identity
Community & Cult Layer
Community Identity

The people who follow TMI know they're part of something. Not a fan club - a perspective. They build real things. They run physical operations. They are tired of being sold software by people who have never dispatched a crew or written a service agreement at midnight.

Language that defines the group: operators, builders, the field, physical work, real companies.
Language to avoid: hustlers, entrepreneurs as a type, the grind.

The Cult Layer

Every strong media brand has something that makes the core audience feel special. For TMI it is access - to how the systems get built, to what other operators are actually dealing with, to the honest version of what AI does and doesn't do for field companies.

The cult layer is the Field Notes newsletter, the FOTF community, and the behind-the-scenes of city lead operations. It is what you get when you go deeper than the Instagram feed.

Experiential Marketing

TMI city leads are not just sales. They are the experiential marketing layer. A city lead walking into a plumbing shop in Dallas is a content moment, a relationship moment, and a distribution moment simultaneously. Document these. Run them as City Lead Diaries content. The physical presence of TMI in a market is part of the brand - and it is content that no SaaS company can replicate.

Metrics
Success Signals

Vanity metrics are follower counts and likes. These are the signals that tell you whether the brand is actually working.

DM volume from right people

Operators, owners, and field managers reaching out. Not "is this free?" but people who already understand what TMI does and want to know if they qualify.

Application source attribution

What percentage of applications reference content. "Saw your reel." "Followed you for three months." "Sent your video to my partner." Track this in every intake call.

Content saves and shares

Saves on Instagram and TikTok beat likes by 10x as a signal. Shares mean they sent it to someone specific. That is the real score. Watch these weekly.

Comment quality

Are operators tagging the person this is clearly for? Are people commenting that this is exactly their situation? Are competitors watching and saying nothing?

City lead warm market rate

When a city lead walks into a shop and the owner already knows TMI, the content is working. Track this as a field data point every week across markets.

Field Notes subscriber growth

Social content drives newsletter subscribers. Newsletter subscribers book calls at 3-5x the rate of cold traffic. Connect these two metrics every week without fail.
