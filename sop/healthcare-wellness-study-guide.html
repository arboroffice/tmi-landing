01 / Foundation

What TMI actually is

TMI stands for Technology Management and Innovation. The brand is TMI Tech AI. Never say "TMI" on its own in public, because another Lafayette firm uses those initials. TMI is a company-building company. It is not an AI agency, not an IT company, not a marketing agency, and not a consultant. AI is one tool, and often not the first thing a company needs. We are based in Lafayette, Louisiana. On site nationwide.

The method is Assess, Design, Build, Integrate, Evolve. The principle is "Preserve what works. Modernize what does not." TMI goes on site, follows the work from first call to payment, builds with the people who will use it, and stays until the system is in use. There is no offshore handoff.

What we are not

  • Not SaaS. We do not sell a tool the client has to configure and manage. The audit works out what the company needs.
  • Not an AI agency. AI is one tool inside the work, not the product.
  • Not consulting. The audit produces a roadmap the client owns, and any work after it is fixed scope and fixed price, in writing before it begins.
  • Not a chatbot. A digital employee is a system that owns a defined, repetitive job. Whether a practice needs one is something the audit decides.

04 / The work

What the work can cover

A client does not pick from a menu. The audit decides which two or three things a practice actually needs. These are the kinds of things it can point to, written in practice terms. Use them to show you understand their world, not as a list of products or promises.

Front desk and follow-up

Digital employees or automation that handle a defined repetitive job, such as answering after-hours messages, texting back missed calls, or confirming appointments. Relates to: missed calls, slow follow-up, an overwhelmed desk.

Scheduling and reminders

A scheduling and reminder workflow that is connected to the rest of the practice instead of run by hand. Relates to: no-shows, manual scheduling.

Intake

Intake that is captured once and moves to where it is needed, instead of being retyped. Relates to: manual intake, double entry.

Bringing patients back

A follow-up system for people who have not been back. Part of the Growth area. Relates to: lapsed patients, a thin calendar.

Reviews and brand

Asking for reviews at the right moment, and a website and brand that match the quality of the practice. Relates to: reputation, new-patient flow.

One record, connected tools

Connecting the tools the practice already pays for so information is not trapped in one system or one person. Relates to: things slipping between people.

Billing follow-up

Invoices, statements, and balance follow-up that run on a schedule instead of on memory. Relates to: collections.

Owner visibility

Business intelligence: the schedule, new patients, revenue, and what needs attention, in one place. Relates to: owner as the bottleneck, no visibility.

On patient information: healthcare data is sensitive and owners will ask. Your line: "That is a fair question. The audit looks at how patient information moves through the practice today, and anything built after it is scoped around that, in writing, before work begins." Do not claim HIPAA compliance, certification, or any specific security or compliance capability. Do not say "HIPAA-compliant" or "HIPAA-certified." If they want detail, that is a question for Mia and Tyler on the Fit Call. A mutual NDA is available on request before anyone talks.

06 / Your job

Your sales playbook

Your scorecard is paid audits. Booked Fit Calls and dated follow-ups are how you get there when an owner is not ready today.

Find

  • Walk your city. Dental groups, multi-provider practices, chiro and PT clinics with several staff, med-spa groups. Favour the established, family-run, multi-location ones.
  • Look for the tells: a phone that rings out, a slow reply to a DM, thin reviews, a "we'll call you back" that never comes. Those are problems you can name.
  • Referrals compound. Every owner and provider you meet knows other practice owners.

Reach out

  • Lead with the problem, not the tech. "I called at 2pm on a Tuesday and it rang out. Does that happen much when the desk is busy?"
  • Use the openers owners recognise: "Everything still comes back to you." "You bought software and the team works around it." "Your company is better than your website makes it look."
  • Let the owner talk about 70% of the time.
  • If a prospect replies, you reply in minutes, not hours.

Sell the audit, or book the next step

  • When the owner is ready, send the tagged payment link for the $5,000 Intelligent Company Audit by text or email, or let them scan the QR. When it is paid, you are credited.
  • If they want to talk first, book the free Fit Call with Mia and Tyler at tmitechai.com/intelligent-company-audit.html, or set a dated follow-up.

Hand off

  • Give Mia and Tyler the context you gathered: which problems you heard, the practice type, size, and anything the owner said mattered to them.
  • Never commit TMI to a timeline, scope, discount, or start date. Never photograph inside a practice or post about a visit without asking.

How to talk about TMI

TMI Tech AI sounds calm, plain, and specific. You represent it. Match it.

Never say

  • "Leverage AI" or anything like it.
  • "We're an AI agency" or "it's an AI chatbot / tool / platform."
  • "Free audit." Only the Fit Call is free.
  • Any result, percentage, ROI, or client story with numbers.

Rule of thumb: talk about the problem and the system that would fix it, never the technology for its own sake. Owners do not buy AI. They buy a front desk that keeps up, a calendar that stays full, and their own time back.
08 / Handling pushback

Objection handling

The shape of each answer: acknowledge, clarify, answer, then ask again for the audit or the next dated step. Never argue.

"It's too expensive."

"Fair. The audit is $5,000 because it is a fixed thing: a score out of 100, a five-page report, and a roadmap you own whatever you decide. If you want to know whether it is worth it for your practice first, the Fit Call is free and takes 20 to 30 minutes. Want me to set that up?"

"Why isn't the audit free?"

"Because a free audit is usually a sales call. Paying for it means we go into the systems and the numbers, and you own a document you can hand to a partner or a banker. The conversation about whether we are a fit is free. That is the Fit Call."

"We already have software / a booking system."

"Good. We keep what works. A lot of practices do not have a software problem, they have tools that do not talk to each other. The audit shows where that is happening and what to connect before anything new gets built."

"Is this a chatbot?"

"No. TMI is not an AI agency. We look at how the whole practice runs. Sometimes the answer includes a system that handles a repetitive job, and sometimes the first fix has nothing to do with AI. The audit decides."

"Will it replace my front desk staff?"

"No. Our line is: do not replace your best people, multiply them. The goal is taking the repetitive part off them so they can look after the patient in front of them."

"What about patient privacy?"

"Fair question, and an important one. The audit looks at how patient information moves through your practice today, and anything built after it is scoped around that in writing. What you tell us stays with TMI, and we can sign a mutual NDA before we talk. For specifics on your setup, that is a question for Mia and Tyler."

"My front desk is fine."

"Might be. One way to check: call your own front desk at your busiest hour and see who picks up. If it is fine, great. If not, that is worth a 20-minute Fit Call."

"Now is not a good time."

"Understood. When would be? I'll put a date on it and follow up then." Then set the date before you leave.

"Send me some info first."

"I will. The audit page is tmitechai.com/intelligent-company-audit.html, and it lays out exactly what you get and what it costs. Can I follow up Thursday to see if you want the audit or the Fit Call?"

09 / After you hand off

The Fit Call and the audit

You do not run either one. Mia and Tyler do. But you set them up to go well.

Booking the Fit Call

The owner submits name, work email, company, phone, and the biggest thing in the way, then picks a time. It takes about a minute. They can also call (337) 450-9795.

What helps on the audit

  • The owner, and whoever actually knows how the work moves. In a practice that is often the office manager or a long-serving front desk lead.
  • Rough numbers if they have them, such as monthly appointments, average visit value, and headcount. The audit works without them.
  • The tools and software they pay for right now.
  • Where the practice leans too hard on the owner or lead provider.
Your line to the prospect: "Nothing to prepare. They research the practice beforehand. If you have rough numbers to hand, the report comes back sharper, but it works without them. Bring whoever knows how the day actually runs."
