Prospecting: channels & scripts

Your highest-trust channel. Walk into the office, ask for the owner by name if you have it. Be brief, be human, name a leak, ask for two minutes. If the owner is out, get the name and the best time, and come back. Dress like you belong on a jobsite, not in a boardroom.

The channel that converts best and costs least. Every client, every friendly no, and every person in your network can hand you a warm door. The trick is simple. You have to actually ask, by name, every time. See the script below.

Show up regularly, not once. Owners buy from people they see twice. Your goal at an event is not to pitch, it is to get two coffees on the calendar. Collect cards, follow up in 48 hours, book the real conversation later.

Fast and scalable. Expect most to not answer. Open with a leak, get to the point in ten seconds, and drive to the call. Call during work hours but not at 7am or right at close. See the branch script below.

Volume channel for owners you cannot reach live. Short, one leak, one question, one link. No paragraphs, no brochure. The whole job of the email is to start a reply, not to sell.

13 / The first conversation

The first conversation & discovery

The goal of the first conversation is one thing. Book the call. You are not here to explain TMI, demo anything, or win a debate about AI. You are here to make an owner feel a leak they already suspected, then hand them 30 free minutes with the founder to see it clearly. If you try to close, you will lose. Open the door and step back.

The framework: Open with a leak. Ask two or three discovery questions. Mirror the pain back in their own words. Book the call. That is the entire play. Four moves. Do not add a fifth.

Discovery questions that surface leaks

You are listening for pain, not delivering a script. Ask a couple, shut up, and let them talk. The moment they describe a leak, you have your booking hook. Group them by what they expose.

About the owner's time

  • If you took a week fully off, would the business run without you calling in?
  • What is the stuff only you can do right now?
  • How much of your day is putting out fires versus actually growing the thing?

About money and billing

  • How confident are you that every job that gets done actually gets billed?
  • How long between finishing work and the invoice going out?
  • Do you know, today, which jobs made money and which lost it?

About lead flow

  • When a call comes in and nobody grabs it, what happens to that lead?
  • How fast does a new lead actually get a call back?
  • When was the last time you reached back out to a past customer?

About the team

  • When something slips, is it usually a person or a process that dropped it?
  • How much lives in one person's head or one person's phone?
  • When you grew, did profit grow with it, or just the chaos?
