The vision

TMI is a media company. The company is the show.

Most companies buy attention. We're building it. TMI isn't a services company that runs ads on the side — it's a media company whose story happens to be true. It runs like a reality show: the people, the arcs, the wins and the low days, the culture, all documented and put out into the world. The audience follows the people — and the people are the company.

Principle 01

Principle 02

The show is the engine, not a side project

How filming daily builds a season for free

You are not choosing between selling, posting, and filming a show. The daily capture is the casting tape, the promo, and the episode footage. The shorts give the show an audience before it airs; the show gives the shorts stakes.

Structure the season around the change. The arc of a person over a season is the plot — the leap, the doubt, the breakthrough, where they end up. The leaderboard is just a clock ticking in the background.
Cast for arcs. The rookie's first close. The closer under pressure. The underdog city no one believed in. The rivalry. The burnout and comeback. Reps aren't just selling — they're playing a role that's genuinely theirs.
Capture once, use forever. Every rep films raw daily and drops it to central. Cut shorts now, assemble episodes later, from the same library. Nothing is shot twice.
Release cadence builds appointment viewing. Shorts keep the characters alive between episodes; episode drops spike everything. YouTube first (own the audience and the revenue), then pitch the packaged season out.
Events

Pull the audience out of the phone and into the room

An online audience is a following. An audience that shows up in person is a movement. Events are how we turn one into the other — and they become the biggest moments of the show. The content earns the handshake; the event is where it happens.

They bring people in

The people who've watched the grind want to be in the room — recruits chasing the life, local owners who feel like they already know the reps, fans who become superfans. Content pulls; the event closes the loop in person.

They're the show's biggest set-pieces

A live event is a season finale. The countdown, the who's-coming, the night itself, the aftermath — that's weeks of content on both sides of a single night.

They make belonging real

Being in the room is status. It turns "I watch these guys" into "I'm part of this." That's the whole distance between an audience and a movement.

They recruit without a pitch

Someone who meets the city leads in person and feels the energy doesn't need to be sold. "Own your city" stops being a video and becomes a room full of proof.

The upside

Where this pays off — without chasing it on camera

None of this is filmed to sell. But an audience that loves the people quietly turns into every one of these. Chase the story; let the commerce be the byproduct.

Warm doors

An owner who's watched the rep grind opens the door already half-sold. The pitch stops being cold. This is the highest-leverage outcome and it's free.

Inbound DMs → booked audit

Owners and referrals slide into the inbox. Route every one to a booked audit through the portal and the rep's Cal.com link. Treat DMs as a queue, not a chat.

Rep booking links + attribution

A trackable link in every bio and every "how to work with me" post. Now you can prove which content books audits, not just which content gets views.

Local authority & referrals

Own the Acadiana trades conversation on Facebook and you become the default recommendation when one owner asks another who to call.

Recruiting is revenue

"Own your city" applications, driven by the content, add reps — which adds pipeline and adds cast. Growth and show both scale off the same funnel.

TMI Media as its own P&L

Sponsorships and brand deals on the channels and the show; eventually licensing the series. Media stops being a marketing cost and becomes a line of business.

The production line

Reps capture, central makes it shine

Reps are not editors. Protect their selling time by splitting capture from post-production cleanly.

Capture — the rep

Phone, raw, consent cleared. Vertical by default. Shoot more than you need; the menu tells you what to point at. Drop everything to one shared place daily.

Cut — central editors

One small team edits, captions, hooks, and templates every clip to the TMI voice. They pull the best moments toward the show library as they go.

Distribute — everywhere it fits

Published to the rep's handles and cross-posted to TMI Media, re-cut per platform. The master library stays owned by the company.

Part II · The camera & the craft

Turning real days into a story worth following

The vision above only works if capture is dead simple in the field. Here's the exact kit, settings, shots, scripts, and edit recipe — built for a DJI Osmo and a phone.

The kit

Osmo Pocket 3 as the A-cam, phone as the B-cam

The Osmo is the workhorse: pocketable, gimbal-stabilized, cinematic in low light, and it flips to vertical with a twist of the screen. The phone covers everything the Osmo can't — a second angle, quick grabs, and native uploads.

DJI Osmo Pocket 3

A-cam · the day gets shot on this
Resolution
4K/60 for everything (60fps gives smooth slow-mo on the grind shots). Drop to 4K/30 to save card space on talky days.
Orientation
Vertical for shorts — rotate the screen. Shoot the show ride-alongs horizontal.
Color
Normal, not D-LogM — speed over grading. Reps aren't colorists.
Tracking
ActiveTrack on for walk-and-talk and solo pieces to camera. It follows your face so you can film yourself hands-free.
Audio
DJI Mic 2 is non-negotiable — clip it on for every piece to camera. It records internally too, so audio survives even out of range.

The phone

B-cam · everything fast & native
Second angle
Prop it for a wide while the Osmo gets the close. Cross-cutting two angles is what makes a clip feel produced.
Grabs
The stuff too quick for the Osmo — a text coming in, a reaction, the truck rolling up.
Proof
Screen-record the leaderboard and screenshot the good DMs. That's social proof you can't reshoot.
Upload & Stories
Native posting, especially Instagram/Facebook Stories — the daily, intimate, unedited layer.
Don't forget

The support kit

Two extra microSD cards (V30 or faster) and a spare charged battery per rep, a small clamp/mount for the phone B-cam, and a weatherproof pouch. Charge and dump footage every night — a dead battery at 6:30am is a lost episode.

The habit

Record-first muscle memory

The Osmo lives in a chest or door pocket, powered on, one click from rolling. The reps who win treat hitting record like putting on a seatbelt — automatic, before the moment, not after they realize it was good.

Field SOP

Hit record when — the capture triggers

Reps don't need to think like filmmakers. They need a short list of moments that always get filmed — and most of them aren't sales moments. Roll on these and the story tells itself.

Morning at the house The drive in The walk-up to a door Right after a no A real win Calling home A milestone at home A low moment Time with the crew Founders talking vision Drive-time thoughts End-of-day reflection
Film the life outside the sale. The house, the team, the moment at home, the quiet drive — that's the story. The sales day is just where a lot of it happens to unfold.
Film the walk-up, not the stranger. The camera can be on you and your face after — you don't need the owner on camera to tell the story. Only feature a prospect or client with a signed release.
Talk to the lens like a buddy in the truck. The hook is the first 1.5 seconds — say where you are and what's really going on. No intros, no "hey guys."
Get the real reaction — including the low ones. The unscripted moment after a hard day is worth more than any polished piece. Narrate the true feeling, don't perform.
Dump by end of day. Footage off the cards and into the shared drop before bed, tagged with the day and city. Same rhythm as logging in the portal.

The change · talking about your own arc
Where you were → the leap → what's different now → where it's going.
"Six months ago I was [X], not sure this would work. Now I'm running a city and my life doesn't look the same. Still early, still figuring it out — but this is the change."
Real and specific beats polished. Name the actual before. Don't oversell the after.

The edit recipe

How central turns raw footage into the TMI look

Reps capture; a small central team cuts. Same recipe every time so the whole feed feels like one brand, whoever shot it.

The cut

One idea, 15–45 seconds

Hook text on the very first frame with motion. Burned-in captions (most watch muted). Cross-cut the Osmo close with the phone wide. Kill every dead second. End on the CTA card.

The look

Consistent TMI signature

Chartreuse accent on captions and the lower-third. A fixed lower-third with rep name · city · leaderboard rank. Clean sans, no gimmicky transitions. It should feel like the same show every time.

Guardrails

What keeps this from blowing up in your face

Documenting real people and real businesses carries real risk. Set these before you scale, not after an incident.

Consent & releases

Anyone clearly on camera — especially prospects and clients — signs a release before you publish them. Don't film inside a business without permission. Louisiana lets you record a conversation you're part of, but publishing an identifiable person is a different bar — get the release. Have counsel draft the show paperwork; this isn't a handshake.

Never burn a prospect

Door footage should make owners want to be featured, not fear it. No mocking, no embarrassing a "no." The camera respects the trades or the whole thing backfires locally.

Client confidentiality

Never reveal a client's real numbers or operations without written sign-off. The transformation content is powerful precisely because it's handled with permission.

Real, not a flex

The low days are part of the story — a sanitized highlight reel kills the whole thing. Stay in the TMI voice: direct, honest, zero hustle-porn. And protect the IP — contract handles and content to the territory, cross-post everything to TMI Media, keep the show as the asset no single exit can take.

The scoreboard

Measure the movement, not the deals

Deals get tracked in the portal, by the business — not here. The content team is chasing something else entirely: are people connecting with these lives enough to come back for the next chapter?

Reach signals

Are people finding the people?

Clips shipped per rep per week · follower growth · watch-through · saves and shares.

Connection signals

Do they actually care?

Returning viewers · comments rooting for a person by name · DMs about the journey · "when's the next one" · recruit interest.
