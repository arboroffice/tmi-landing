Architecture

Single-agent is legacy.
We run a swarm.

Single-agent AI hits a ceiling fast. One model handling dispatch, compliance, billing, and scheduling creates bottlenecks — and when it fails, everything stops. That's a fragile architecture.

Modern backends use Multi-Agent Systems — a coordinated swarm of specialized units, each owning a defined domain. They run in parallel, hand off to each other, and escalate to humans only when decisions exceed their authority. The operation doesn't wait on any single thread.

"Every agent has a defined scope. The platform manages priority, conflict resolution, and human escalation when decisions exceed agent authority."

How it works

Three principles.
One architecture.

The design decisions that separate an AI operating system from a collection of AI tools.

01

Specialization

Each agent owns one domain.

Dispatch agents know crews, certifications, and routes. Compliance agents know regulations and filing windows. Billing agents know rates, contracts, and approval chains. Specialization means depth — not breadth spread thin across a single overloaded model that forgets context as tasks pile up.

02

Parallel Execution

No waiting. No bottlenecks.

While one agent closes a work order, another drafts the invoice, a third checks the compliance log, and a fourth queues the next dispatch. The operation runs all threads simultaneously — not one at a time. Sequential AI is just a faster human. Parallel AI is a different kind of infrastructure.

03

Orchestrated Handoff

Agents coordinate. Humans decide what matters.

When an agent reaches a decision that exceeds its authority — a large spend approval, a regulatory exception, a novel situation with no precedent — it escalates with full context. Everything else closes without a human touching it. The operation doesn't ask for permission on routine work. It asks for judgment on the things that actually need it.
