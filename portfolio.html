The leaks owners feel first

Four problems that quietly cost owners the most.

These come up in the first ten minutes of almost every audit, and they are the ones a system pays for itself by fixing.

Instant estimates

The quote that took three days lost the job.

The story

A home-service owner watched good leads go cold while quotes sat in someone's truck. The fast competitor down the road got there first, every time.

What we built

We built instant estimates off your price book. The customer gets a real, itemized price with financing attached while they are still on the phone or standing at the door.

Quotes in minutes, not days
The owner bottleneck

The business could not run without the owner in the room.

The story

Every dispatch call, every price, every approval ran through one person. He had not taken a real week off in years, and the day fell apart whenever he tried.

What we built

We built a command layer and a crew of agents that run the day, dispatch, quoting, follow-up, and billing, with the owner approving only what actually matters.

First week off in years, nothing broke
Unknown job margin

He found out a job lost money months after it was done.

The story

Estimates were guesses, and actual labor, materials, and rework never got tracked against them. The owner could not tell a profitable job from a money pit until the books closed, if ever.

What we built

We built live job costing that tracks everything, every hour, part, and change order, against the estimate, by job, by crew, and by service type, updated as the work happens.

Margin on every job, in real time
Parts & reordering

Techs kept showing up to jobs missing the part.

The story

Nobody knew what was on the trucks or the shelves. Crews drove back for parts, jobs stalled, and stock either ran out mid-job or piled up as dead cash in the warehouse.

What we built

We built parts and inventory tracking that reorders itself. It knows what is on every truck and shelf, ties each part to the job, and reorders automatically when stock drops below par.

No empty-handed trips, no dead stock
