# TMI OS — Offer & Segment Architecture

The OS is the engine. On top of it sit two axes: **who it's for** (segments) and **how deep TMI goes with them** (the offer ladder). Same OS, four doors in, four heights of engagement. This is the shape of TMI as a SaaS company, not a feature list.

---

## The four segments (who comes in)

1. **Businesses** — owner-led companies. Install their intelligent company so it runs on systems, not on the owner.
2. **Creators / personal brands** — the backend ops system behind an audience: community, content pipeline, brand deals, monetization.
3. **Agencies / AI Installers** — operators who install AI and the OS *for other businesses*, using TMI as the engine underneath. They run many client workspaces. This is the platform play: TMI becomes the thing others sell.
4. **Private Equity / Portfolio owners** — install the OS across a portfolio, standardize operations across holdings, and raise enterprise value and the exit multiple, with one roll-up view over every company.

Each segment is a different front door and a different first-run, but they all resolve to the same OS.

---

## The offer ladder (how you engage — ascending ticket)

1. **Self-serve OS (SaaS).** Subscription. You build your own company in the OS. Product-led, lowest ticket, highest volume. The onboarding we just shipped (website or handles → approve → built) is this door.
2. **Done-with-you / Personalization.** TMI's development team customizes and installs it for you: bespoke workers, real integrations wired to your books and tools, custom builds. Higher ticket, delivered. The OS already has the "Build with TMI" request path; this is the paid version of it.
3. **1-1 Strategy.** A TMI strategist works with you on the transformation itself — the plan, the priorities, the operating model. Advisory on top of the software.
4. **Founder-level / Partner.** The top: direct work with founders, elite engagement, and the **Installer / Partner program** for agencies and PE (multi-client, white-label, revenue share, certification).

The self-serve tier is bought in-app. Everything above it is application- or sales-led — the OS just needs a clean "request / apply" path into each, which the Apply page and Build-with-TMI already model.

## The AI Installer / Partner model (the platform play)

The line "you can be an AI installer and use our services to install AI for others" is a distinct product track, not a plan tier:

- **Certified TMI Installers** — agencies, consultants, fractional operators who deliver AI + the OS to their own clients.
- **A partner workspace** — manage many client OS instances from one place: create a client, run their build, monitor their command centers, hand off or co-manage.
- **White-label option**, **revenue share**, and **certification** (ties into TMI Institute).
- **Levels:** Advisor → Builder → Operator → Enterprise Partner.

This is how "install AI for others" scales: TMI stops being only a product you buy and becomes the engine other operators sell on top of.

## The PE / Portfolio model

- **Roll-up view** across every holding: each company's Intelligence Score, owner-dependence, and live metrics on one screen.
- **Standardize the operating system** across acquisitions so every company runs the same way — the thing that raises the multiple.
- **Diligence mode:** score a target's operational intelligence before acquiring, and model the post-close build.

---

## What this needs from the product (structural, not features)

Most of the above is packaging and sales. Three things are genuine structure the OS needs:

1. **Workspace modes.** Today one login = one company. Add:
   - *Solo* (business or creator) — what exists now.
   - *Installer* (agency) — one account, many client workspaces, with a switcher and client management.
   - *Portfolio* (PE) — one account, many companies, with a roll-up view across them.
   This is the biggest lift and it unlocks segments 3 and 4.
2. **Plans + the offer ladder, surfaced.** Self-serve tiers bought in-app (needs the subscription billing from the OS roadmap), plus a clean "apply / talk to us" path into DWY, 1-1, and Founder.
3. **Partner onboarding + dashboard.** How an installer signs up, gets certified, and manages clients; how a PE firm adds holdings.

Everything else — tier names, prices, which segment to lead with, the DWY and 1-1 scopes — is packaging and GTM you own.

---

## Decisions for Mia (before building)

1. **Which segment leads?** My recommendation: businesses + creators self-serve now (already have the door), and stand up the **Installer** track next, because it turns customers into a distribution channel.
2. **Is white-label / installer near-term or later?** It's the highest-leverage but also the biggest structural build (workspace modes).
3. **Self-serve price points** for the plan tiers (the rest are application/quote).
4. **Where do the higher offers live** — inside the OS ("Upgrade to done-with-you") or on the marketing site (Apply, segmented)?

---

## Recommended first build

Not features — **workspace modes + the offer surface.** Concretely:
- Add the **Installer workspace** (multi-client) as the structural unlock for agencies, and the **Portfolio** roll-up for PE.
- Surface the **offer ladder** in-app: self-serve plans (with billing) and an "apply for done-with-you / strategy / founder" path.
- Segmented **front doors** on the marketing site and in OS signup (Business / Creator / Agency / Investor), each routing to the right first-run and the right offer.

That makes TMI one OS that sells to all four segments at four price heights, with agencies and PE as multipliers rather than one-off customers.
