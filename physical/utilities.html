The Problem

The operational gap in utilities

Large service territories, aging infrastructure, regulatory complexity, and dispersed field crews create coordination and compliance pressure that generic software wasn't designed for.

01

Field crews dispersed across territories without visibility

Dozens of technicians working across hundreds of square miles, each with their own work orders, tools, and schedules — coordinated by dispatchers who are managing everything on phone calls and spreadsheets that don't tell you where anything actually is.

02

Compliance documentation that never stops

NERC, EPA, FERC, and state PUC requirements produce a continuous stream of documentation obligations. When it's tracked manually — across emails, binders, and disconnected systems — something always slips, and the penalties are real.

03

Aging infrastructure with no predictive visibility

Transformers, lines, meters, and substation equipment that fails on its own schedule — not yours. Without systematic asset health tracking, maintenance is reactive, outages are surprises, and capital planning is guesswork.
