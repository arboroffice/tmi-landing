The Problem

You're leaving money in every job and you know it.

Parts not logged. Add-ons not priced. Commercial maintenance contracts tracked by memory. Most plumbing shops are running wide open and still wondering why the margins don't reflect the volume.

01

Running out of parts mid-job is not a supply problem

It's a system problem. When techs pull parts from their van without logging it, you don't know what's depleted until someone can't finish a job. A $14 fitting costs you a second dispatch, a customer callback, and two hours of labor. Most shops lose 8-12% of job revenue to untracked material consumption every month.

02

Flat-rate pricing confusion is costing you close rate and margin

Some techs price by the hour. Some use flat rate. Some have the book memorized. Some don't. The result is inconsistent quotes, customer confusion on price differences, and margin variance of 15-25% across the same job type depending on who shows up. That's not a training problem -- it's a pricing system problem.

03

Commercial maintenance contracts are recurring revenue you're not actually collecting

You've got 50 commercial accounts that are supposed to get quarterly check-ins. Half are behind. The invoices go out late because someone has to manually pull the account history and build the visit record. Revenue that should be automatic is instead manual -- and it shows in the collection rate.
