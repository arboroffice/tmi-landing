The Problem

A busy practice is not the same as a profitable one.

Most dental practices are fully booked and under-producing. The schedule looks healthy. The P&L tells a different story. The gap lives in treatment plan follow-up, recall systems, and a new patient pipeline that depends entirely on hope.

01

Treatment plan acceptance is low and the follow-up is a sticky note

The average dental practice has $80,000 to $150,000 in unaccepted treatment sitting in the system at any given time. The follow-up process is a coordinator with a list and good intentions. That's not a system.

02

Hygiene recall is inconsistent at best

Patients get one postcard and nothing else. If they don't call back, they're gone. A practice with 1,200 active patients and a 60% recall rate is leaving hundreds of appointments and tens of thousands on the table every year.

03

The new patient pipeline is word of mouth and a Google listing

When a new patient searches for a dentist, the practice that responds first and follows up consistently wins. Most practices have no system for either. They rely on existing patients to send referrals and hope the phone rings.
