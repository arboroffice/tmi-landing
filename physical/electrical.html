The Problem

Commercial work is complex. Residential is fast. Your systems need to handle both.

A residential service call and a commercial project with 20 active permits have nothing in common operationally. Most electrical shops run them with the same manual process -- and it shows in the callbacks, the missed inspections, and the job costing that never matches the estimate.

01

Permit tracking is non-negotiable and almost always manual

On a commercial project with multiple trades, you might have 12 active permits across two jurisdictions. Each has its own inspection schedule, documentation requirement, and expiration timeline. Missing a status check costs a week. Missing an inspection means a re-inspection fee, a schedule delay, and a GC who calls your competition next time. Most shops track this in a spreadsheet that one person maintains.

02

Commercial job costing is complex and almost always wrong at close

Labor burden, material escalation, change orders, and phased billing -- commercial electrical work is a job costing exercise from start to finish. Most contractors reconcile at the end of the job and discover the margin was 8 points lower than estimated. By then, the next job has been quoted using the same assumptions.

03

Residential and commercial scheduling don't mix in the same dispatch logic

A residential service call needs to be at the customer's house in 2-4 hours. A commercial project crew needs to be sequenced around the GC's schedule, the inspection calendar, and material deliveries. Most dispatch systems handle one or the other. Running both in the same spreadsheet means one always suffers.
