The Problem

The recurring service model breaks without tight systems.

Pest control companies run on route density, chemical compliance, and retention. When any one of those slips -- a sloppy route, a missed EPA record, a customer who wasn't followed up -- it compounds across hundreds of accounts.

01

Route density is everything -- and most routes are 20% looser than they need to be

Every extra mile between stops costs time, fuel, and technician capacity. Routes built manually drift. New customers added in the wrong sequence. Service windows scheduled without regard to geography. The waste is invisible until you actually map it.

02

Chemical inventory tracking is both operational and a regulatory requirement

EPA record-keeping for restricted-use pesticides isn't optional. Most shops track it on paper or in a spreadsheet that someone updates inconsistently. An audit finds a gap and the fine is real. The fix is a system that captures usage at the point of application, not at the end of the week.

03

Seasonal spikes break manual scheduling every spring and fall

Call volume doubles in mosquito season. Termite inspection requests spike in spring. The same manual scheduling process that barely handles normal volume collapses when demand surges -- and you lose jobs to competitors who answer faster.
