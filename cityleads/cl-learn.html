Who to target

The right business

Trades and field service companies with 4-50 employees. Owner-operated. They have crews in the field every day. They're busy enough to have problems but not so big they have a full IT department already.

The right owner

Owners who are clearly still in the business — you see their truck out front, they're at the shop, they answer the phone. Avoid absentee owners or holding companies. The person who feels the pain every day is who you want.

Best industries

HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing, landscaping, painting, concrete, welding, heavy equipment. Not restaurants. Not retail. Not office businesses.

The walk-in

What to say walking in

Ask for the owner by name if you have it. If not, ask "Is the owner or manager around?" When you meet them:

"Hey, my name is [Name], I work with a tech company called TMI — we help trades businesses like yours get their scheduling and job tracking off spreadsheets. I'm not here to pitch anything today, I just had a quick question about how you're running things — do you have two minutes?"

What NOT to say

Don't say "software." Don't say "AI." Don't say "solution" or "platform." Don't start with a feature list. You're having a conversation, not giving a demo. The goal of visit one is to find out if they have a problem worth solving.

The diagnostic question

After small talk, ask: "How do you currently keep track of what your crews are doing each day?" Their answer tells you everything. If they say "text messages" or "we just figure it out" — that's your opening.

When a lead is warm

Signals to look for

They asked follow-up questions. They said "yeah that's actually a problem for us." They invited you back. They mentioned a specific pain more than once. They asked about price.

The handoff signal

When an owner says "I'd want to see how this actually works" or asks "what would it cost?" — that's your cue to set up the audit with Mia or Tyler. Do not try to demo it yourself. Say: "That's exactly where I'd bring in our team — they do a 20-minute audit and walk you through exactly what it would look like for your operation."

Privacy rules

What stays private

Don't share owner details with anyone outside TMI. Don't post photos of the inside of a business without permission. Don't share deal sizes or client names on social. Everything you log in this app stays inside TMI.
